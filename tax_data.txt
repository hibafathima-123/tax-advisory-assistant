===========================
INDIAN INCOME TAX GUIDE 2024-25
===========================

--- SECTION 1: BASIC DEFINITIONS ---

WHAT IS INCOME TAX?
Income tax is a direct tax levied by the Government of India on the income earned by individuals, Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs), companies, and other entities during a financial year (April 1 to March 31).

WHAT IS TAXABLE INCOME?
Taxable income is the portion of your total income on which you are required to pay tax. It is calculated as:
Taxable Income = Gross Total Income - Deductions (under Chapter VI-A) - Exemptions

WHAT IS A FINANCIAL YEAR (FY)?
A Financial Year (FY) runs from April 1 to March 31 of the following year. For example, FY 2024-25 runs from April 1, 2024 to March 31, 2025.

WHAT IS AN ASSESSMENT YEAR (AY)?
An Assessment Year (AY) is the year immediately following the Financial Year, during which you file your tax return. For income earned in FY 2024-25, the Assessment Year is AY 2025-26.

WHAT IS A DEDUCTION?
A deduction is an amount that is subtracted from your gross total income before calculating tax. Deductions reduce your taxable income, thereby reducing your tax liability.

WHAT IS AN EXEMPTION?
An exemption is an income that is not subject to tax at all. Examples include HRA exemption and agricultural income.

WHAT IS TDS (TAX DEDUCTED AT SOURCE)?
TDS is a mechanism where tax is deducted at the source of income by the payer. For example, your employer deducts TDS from your salary every month and deposits it with the government on your behalf.

WHAT IS PAN (PERMANENT ACCOUNT NUMBER)?
PAN is a 10-digit alphanumeric identifier issued by the Income Tax Department. It is mandatory for filing income tax returns and for all financial transactions above specified limits.

WHAT IS ITR (INCOME TAX RETURN)?
An ITR is a form that taxpayers file with the Income Tax Department to report their income, deductions, and tax paid. The due date for individuals is usually July 31 of the Assessment Year.

--- SECTION 2: INCOME TAX SLABS (OLD REGIME) FY 2024-25 ---

The Old Tax Regime allows various deductions and exemptions and has the following tax slabs for individuals below 60 years of age:

Income up to Rs. 2,50,000 - No Tax (NIL)
Income from Rs. 2,50,001 to Rs. 5,00,000 - 5% tax
Income from Rs. 5,00,001 to Rs. 10,00,000 - 20% tax
Income above Rs. 10,00,000 - 30% tax

For Senior Citizens (aged 60 to 79 years):
Income up to Rs. 3,00,000 - No Tax (NIL)
Income from Rs. 3,00,001 to Rs. 5,00,000 - 5% tax
Income from Rs. 5,00,001 to Rs. 10,00,000 - 20% tax
Income above Rs. 10,00,000 - 30% tax

For Super Senior Citizens (aged 80 years and above):
Income up to Rs. 5,00,000 - No Tax (NIL)
Income from Rs. 5,00,001 to Rs. 10,00,000 - 20% tax
Income above Rs. 10,00,000 - 30% tax

SURCHARGE on Old Regime:
10% surcharge on income between Rs. 50 lakh to Rs. 1 crore
15% surcharge on income between Rs. 1 crore to Rs. 2 crore
25% surcharge on income between Rs. 2 crore to Rs. 5 crore
37% surcharge on income above Rs. 5 crore

HEALTH AND EDUCATION CESS: 4% on (Tax + Surcharge) for all taxpayers.

REBATE UNDER SECTION 87A (Old Regime):
If your total taxable income is up to Rs. 5,00,000, you get a tax rebate of Rs. 12,500 or the actual tax payable, whichever is lower. This effectively means ZERO TAX for income up to Rs. 5,00,000 in the old regime.

--- SECTION 3: INCOME TAX SLABS (NEW REGIME) FY 2024-25 ---

The New Tax Regime (Default from FY 2023-24) has lower tax rates but does NOT allow most deductions and exemptions. It was updated in Budget 2024.

Income up to Rs. 3,00,000 - No Tax (NIL)
Income from Rs. 3,00,001 to Rs. 7,00,000 - 5% tax
Income from Rs. 7,00,001 to Rs. 10,00,000 - 10% tax
Income from Rs. 10,00,001 to Rs. 12,00,000 - 15% tax
Income from Rs. 12,00,001 to Rs. 15,00,000 - 20% tax
Income above Rs. 15,00,000 - 30% tax

STANDARD DEDUCTION under New Regime: Rs. 75,000 (increased from Rs. 50,000 in Budget 2024)

REBATE UNDER SECTION 87A (New Regime):
If your total taxable income is up to Rs. 7,00,000, you get a full tax rebate. This means ZERO TAX for income up to Rs. 7,00,000 under the new regime.

SURCHARGE under New Regime: Maximum surcharge is capped at 25% (compared to 37% in old regime).

--- SECTION 4: SECTION 80C - THE MOST POPULAR DEDUCTION ---

Section 80C allows a deduction of up to Rs. 1,50,000 per year from your taxable income. This is available ONLY under the Old Tax Regime.

Eligible investments and payments under 80C:

1. Employee Provident Fund (EPF) - Contribution by employee
2. Public Provident Fund (PPF) - Maximum Rs. 1.5 lakh per year, 15-year lock-in
3. National Savings Certificate (NSC) - 5-year lock-in
4. Equity Linked Savings Scheme (ELSS) - Mutual fund with 3-year lock-in (shortest lock-in)
5. Life Insurance Premium - Paid for self, spouse, or children
6. Unit Linked Insurance Plan (ULIP) premiums
7. 5-year Fixed Deposit with banks or post offices
8. Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY) - For girl child below 10 years
9. Senior Citizen Savings Scheme (SCSS)
10. National Pension System (NPS) - Tier I contributions (partial)
11. Home Loan Principal Repayment
12. Tuition Fees for up to 2 children (full-time education in India)
13. Stamp duty and registration charges for house property

Important: The combined limit for all 80C investments is Rs. 1,50,000. You cannot claim more than Rs. 1.5 lakh even if your total investments exceed this amount.

--- SECTION 5: SECTION 80D - HEALTH INSURANCE DEDUCTION ---

Section 80D provides deduction for health insurance premiums. Available under OLD REGIME only.

For Self, Spouse, and Dependent Children:
- Premium paid for health insurance: Up to Rs. 25,000
- If any insured person is a senior citizen (60+): Up to Rs. 50,000

For Parents:
- Premium paid for parents' health insurance: Up to Rs. 25,000
- If parents are senior citizens (60+): Up to Rs. 50,000

MAXIMUM DEDUCTION SCENARIOS:
- If you are below 60 and parents are below 60: Rs. 25,000 + Rs. 25,000 = Rs. 50,000
- If you are below 60 and parents are senior citizens: Rs. 25,000 + Rs. 50,000 = Rs. 75,000
- If you are a senior citizen and parents are also senior citizens: Rs. 50,000 + Rs. 50,000 = Rs. 1,00,000

Preventive Health Check-up: Within the above limits, Rs. 5,000 can be claimed for preventive health check-ups (payment in cash is also allowed for this).

--- SECTION 6: OTHER IMPORTANT DEDUCTIONS (OLD REGIME) ---

SECTION 80CCD(1B) - NPS ADDITIONAL DEDUCTION:
An additional deduction of Rs. 50,000 is available for contributions to National Pension System (NPS) Tier-I account. This is OVER AND ABOVE the Rs. 1.5 lakh limit of Section 80C.

SECTION 80E - EDUCATION LOAN INTEREST:
Deduction on interest paid on education loan for higher studies. No upper limit on the amount. Available for 8 years (from the year you start repaying) or until the interest is fully paid, whichever is earlier. The loan must be taken from a financial institution or approved charitable institution.

SECTION 80G - DONATIONS:
Deductions for donations made to certain approved funds and charitable institutions. 100% or 50% deduction depending on the institution. Some donations have a 10% of Adjusted Gross Total Income limit.

SECTION 24(b) - HOME LOAN INTEREST:
Deduction of up to Rs. 2,00,000 on interest paid on home loan for a self-occupied house. For a rented-out property, the entire interest is deductible without limit (subject to loss set-off rules).

SECTION 80TTA - SAVINGS ACCOUNT INTEREST:
Deduction of up to Rs. 10,000 on interest earned from savings accounts in banks, post offices, or cooperative societies. For senior citizens, Section 80TTB allows deduction up to Rs. 50,000 (including FD interest).

HOUSE RENT ALLOWANCE (HRA) EXEMPTION:
HRA received from employer is exempt to the extent of the lowest of:
1. Actual HRA received
2. 50% of basic salary (for metros: Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata) or 40% (for non-metros)
3. Actual rent paid minus 10% of basic salary

STANDARD DEDUCTION FOR SALARIED EMPLOYEES:
Rs. 50,000 flat deduction for salaried employees under OLD regime (Rs. 75,000 under NEW regime).

LEAVE TRAVEL ALLOWANCE (LTA):
Exemption for travel expenses for self and family within India. Can be claimed twice in a block of 4 years.

--- SECTION 7: CAPITAL GAINS TAX ---

WHAT ARE CAPITAL GAINS?
Capital gains are profits earned from the sale of capital assets like property, stocks, mutual funds, gold, etc.

SHORT-TERM CAPITAL GAINS (STCG):
- Equity shares/equity mutual funds (held less than 12 months): Taxed at 20% (increased from 15% in Budget 2024)
- Other assets like property, gold (held less than 24 or 36 months): Taxed as per your income tax slab

LONG-TERM CAPITAL GAINS (LTCG):
- Equity shares/equity mutual funds (held more than 12 months): Gains above Rs. 1,25,000 taxed at 12.5% (without indexation). Exemption limit increased from Rs. 1 lakh to Rs. 1.25 lakh in Budget 2024.
- Property (held more than 24 months): Taxed at 12.5% without indexation OR 20% with indexation (taxpayer can choose the more beneficial option for assets purchased before July 23, 2024). For assets purchased on or after July 23, 2024: 12.5% without indexation only.
- Debt Mutual Funds (purchased after April 1, 2023): Taxed as per income slab (no LTCG benefit)

INDEXATION BENEFIT:
Indexation adjusts the purchase price of an asset for inflation using the Cost Inflation Index (CII), reducing your taxable capital gains.

--- SECTION 8: WHICH TAX REGIME TO CHOOSE? ---

CHOOSE NEW REGIME IF:
- You have fewer investments/deductions
- Your income is below Rs. 15 lakh with standard deduction
- You are a salaried employee with income up to Rs. 7,50,000 (after Rs. 75,000 standard deduction, taxable income is Rs. 6,75,000 - zero tax)
- You prefer simplicity and lower compliance burden

CHOOSE OLD REGIME IF:
- You have home loan (principal + interest deductions)
- You invest heavily in 80C instruments (EPF, PPF, ELSS, LIC)
- You pay significant health insurance premiums (80D)
- You pay HRA (rented accommodation)
- Your total deductions exceed Rs. 3.75 lakh

BREAK-EVEN ANALYSIS:
For income of Rs. 10 lakh: If your deductions exceed Rs. 3,75,000, old regime saves more tax.
For income of Rs. 15 lakh: If your deductions exceed Rs. 4,08,000 approximately, old regime is better.

--- SECTION 9: HOW TO FILE INCOME TAX RETURN ---

STEP 1: Collect all documents
- Form 16 (from employer)
- Bank statements
- Investment proofs (PPF, ELSS, LIC receipts)
- Home loan certificate
- Rent receipts
- Form 26AS and AIS (Annual Information Statement)

STEP 2: Choose the correct ITR form
- ITR-1 (Sahaj): Salaried individuals with income up to Rs. 50 lakh
- ITR-2: Capital gains, foreign income
- ITR-3: Business/professional income
- ITR-4 (Sugam): Presumptive business income

STEP 3: Visit income tax portal
Go to incometax.gov.in, login with PAN and password.

STEP 4: File and verify
After filing, e-verify using Aadhaar OTP, Net Banking, or send signed ITR-V to CPC Bangalore within 30 days.

DUE DATE: July 31 of the Assessment Year for most individuals.
LATE FILING FEE: Rs. 1,000 if income is below Rs. 5 lakh; Rs. 5,000 if income is above Rs. 5 lakh (under Section 234F).

--- SECTION 10: ADVANCE TAX ---

WHO NEEDS TO PAY ADVANCE TAX?
If your total tax liability (after TDS) exceeds Rs. 10,000 in a financial year, you must pay advance tax.

ADVANCE TAX SCHEDULE:
By June 15 - 15% of estimated tax
By September 15 - 45% of estimated tax
By December 15 - 75% of estimated tax
By March 15 - 100% of estimated tax

INTEREST FOR NON-PAYMENT:
Section 234B: 1% per month if advance tax paid is less than 90% of total tax.
Section 234C: 1% per month for deferment of advance tax installments.

--- SECTION 11: FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS ---

Q: Is agricultural income taxable in India?
A: No, agricultural income is completely exempt from income tax under Section 10(1). However, it is used for rate purposes (to determine the applicable tax rate on non-agricultural income) if it exceeds Rs. 5,000.

Q: I am 90 years old and my income is less than Rs. 4,00,000. Should I pay tax?
A: If you are 90 years old, you are a Super Senior Citizen under the old tax regime. Under the old regime, income up to Rs. 5,00,000 for Super Senior Citizens is NIL, so income below Rs. 4,00,000 results in no tax. Under the new regime, the Section 87A rebate means taxable income up to Rs. 7,00,000 results in zero tax. So, based on this knowledge base, income below Rs. 4,00,000 means no tax in either regime.

Q: What is the tax on gifts received?
A: Gifts received exceeding Rs. 50,000 in a year are taxable under "Income from Other Sources" unless received from relatives (spouse, siblings, parents, grandparents, etc.) or on occasions like marriage.

Q: Can I switch between old and new tax regimes?
A: Salaried individuals can switch between regimes every year when filing ITR. Business owners can switch only once (from new to old) and cannot switch back.

Q: What happens if I miss the ITR filing deadline?
A: You can file a belated return up to December 31 of the Assessment Year with a late fee. After December 31, you can only file if you receive a notice from the Income Tax Department.

Q: Is PF withdrawal taxable?
A: EPF withdrawal is tax-free if you have completed 5 continuous years of service. If withdrawn before 5 years, it is taxable.

Q: What is Form 26AS?
A: Form 26AS is a consolidated tax statement available on the income tax portal. It shows all TDS deducted by various deductors, advance tax paid, and other tax payments made against your PAN.

Q: What is the tax on fixed deposit interest?
A: FD interest is fully taxable as per your income tax slab rate. Banks deduct TDS at 10% if interest exceeds Rs. 40,000 (Rs. 50,000 for senior citizens) in a year. Senior citizens can claim deduction under Section 80TTB up to Rs. 50,000.

Q: How is rent income taxed?
A: Rental income is taxed under "Income from House Property". A standard deduction of 30% is allowed on net annual value, along with deduction for property taxes paid and home loan interest.
